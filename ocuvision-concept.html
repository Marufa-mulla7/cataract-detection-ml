Intended Use

Screens fundus (retinal) photographs for five conditions — Normal, Cataract, Glaucoma, Age-related Macular Degeneration (AMD), and Diabetic Retinopathy — and pairs each prediction with Grad-CAM explainability, Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty, and a cross-model second-opinion check. This is a student academic research project, not a certified medical diagnostic device. It has not undergone regulatory review or prospective clinical validation, and has not been tested on real hospital equipment.

Out of scope: external/anterior eye photographs. Of the five conditions above, only Cataract has any visible external signature — Glaucoma, Diabetic Retinopathy, and AMD are retinal/optic-nerve conditions that cannot be diagnosed from a non-fundus photo under any circumstances.

4,600 images total, split into train / validation / test (3,434 / 583 / 583). Validation and test sets contain real images only; augmented images are in the training split only. All test-set figures below are computed on the 583 held-out test images.

⚠ Honest caveat: 712 of AMD's 900 images (84% of its training images) are augmented copies of only 132 real training photos (188 unique real AMD sources overall), and Diabetic Retinopathy is the largest class at 1,000 of 4,600 images (21.7%). Both facts affect how the per-class results below should be read. See Known Limitations.

Cost-Weighted Evaluation

Re-scores primary-model errors with an asymmetric cost: missed disease = 10, disease-confused-with-disease = 5, false alarm = 1.

Plain accuracy95.2% (555/583)
Cost-weighted score0.9744
Missed disease (disease → Normal)9 cases
False alarm (Normal → disease)9 cases

The cost-weighted score sits close to plain accuracy, but 9 of the model's 28 test-set errors (32%) are missed-disease cases (Cataract or Glaucoma called Normal) — the most clinically dangerous outcome, where a diseased eye is called Normal. When two independently-trained models disagree, treat the result with extra caution; that's why disagreement is surfaced in every real analysis result.

Known Limitations
  1. AMD's training data is 84% augmented (712 of 844 training images are augmented copies of only 132 real training photos; 188 unique real sources overall), so its near-perfect F1 may partly reflect recognizing "known source image, transformed" rather than fully generalizable AMD features. Its test set has only 28 images.
  2. Diabetic Retinopathy is the largest class (1,000 of 4,600 images, 21.7%). Its perfect internal scores likely reflect single-source imaging conditions; an external DR image was classified as Normal, so internal results must not be read as real-world performance.
  3. No validation on real hospital-sourced images. The model hasn't been tested on images from different cameras, lighting, or patient populations than its Kaggle training sources.
  4. Confidence is not calibrated. In a 100-image check the Monte Carlo Dropout flag never fired, even for the two errors (99.6% and 99.9% confidence), so the review flag also uses model disagreement and confidence below 70%.
  5. External eye photographs are out of scope. Only fundus (retinal) images are analyzed — external cataract clouding, conjunctivitis, etc. are not detected.
  6. Grad-CAM is CNN-specific and would not directly apply if a Vision Transformer were added in future work.
  7. Mixed sources, unconfirmed patient-level split. Classes come from different public datasets, so the models may partly learn source or camera features; leakage was checked at the parent-image level, not by patient.
